# Behavioral Profile

Three formal assessments are on file in Drive, plus Casey's own answers in the /setup interview (2026-10-17):
- **Everything DiSC Workplace** (June 2021, through the Cornell Executive MBA): style **D**, in the middle of the Dominance region
- **CliftonStrengths** (Gallup, August 2022): top five themes **Achiever, Arranger, Futuristic, Significance, Self-Assurance**
- **Myers-Briggs:** **ENTJ**, "Field General" (Casey's own recollection; no report on file)

## Overview
Casey is a **builder and a field general**: a results-first operator who takes charge, sets direction, organizes people and resources around it, and drives to a finish. All three assessments agree: DiSC D (decisive, results-driven), Achiever and Arranger (relentless follow-through, orchestrating many moving parts), Futuristic (sets the vision), and ENTJ (commands toward the objective). In his own words, he has grown from being responsible to being **accountable**: he creates and sponsors strategies rather than only executing other people's.

## Strongest Behaviors
- **Aligns people around a common goal:** thrives when teams come together on shared objectives; actively shuts down bickering and backbiting
- **Situational decision-making:** moves fast when the path is clear, gathers data when it isn't, and when necessary moves forward and collects data to correct course
- **Force multiplier:** raises the output of engineering and delivery organizations (5x velocity, 70% fewer infrastructure bottlenecks)
- **Executive alignment:** works directly with executives and bank C-suites on strategy, governance, and roadmaps
- **Cost and revenue focus:** finds money in underused resources (50% cloud savings, $30M+ ARR rescued)

## How You Work Best
- High-growth environments of roughly 100-1,000 people where clarity, speed, and structure matter; also open to large public companies with room to build
- Owning and sponsoring the strategy, close to executive leadership
- Hybrid, with some in-person time with the team
- Teams that collaborate toward shared goals; drained by politics, bickering, and toxic backbiting

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)
- **Not as technical as he could be** (Casey, 2026-10-17). He relies on deeply technical people, such as the principal-engineer architects on his team, and trusts their depth. Frame it as a deliberate leadership choice: he hires and empowers strong technical leaders while owning the outcome, and knows enough to ask the right questions
- **DiSC watch-out (from the report, not Casey's words):** under pressure a D style can come across as forceful or impatient. His team describes him as supportive and encouraging, which is the counterweight to cite if asked

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a job posting mentions these keywords, it's a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "build from the ground up", "0 to 1", "stand up a new function"
- "own the strategy", "accountable for outcomes", "drive results"
- "scale", "hypergrowth", "operating rhythm", "chief of staff"
- "cross-functional", "ambiguity", "drive alignment across executives"
- "cost efficiency", "AI enablement", "M&A integration"

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not deal-breaker):
- Pure maintenance or "keep the lights on" roles
- Roles that only execute someone else's plan, with no ownership of strategy
- Deep hands-on technical roles: architecture ownership, writing production code, ML research
- Cultures described as consensus-heavy or slow to decide
